WeeklyHealthReport privacy policy
Effective 4 October 2026

WeeklyHealthReport is a local health-reporting and food-recording app developed by Sertan Yamaner / qbq Solutions Ltd. Contact: proxy@sertan.com. We do not operate an app account service, analytics service, advertising system or backend that receives your health records. We do not sell your data, track you across apps, or use your health data for advertising or model training.

Apple Health

With your permission, the app reads selected HealthKit information to calculate reports on your iPhone. This can include body measurements, glucose, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, heart rate and variability, cardiorespiratory fitness, steps, walking/running distance, sleep, activity, workouts, selected dietary data and, on supported systems, individually selected medications. Access is read-only: the app does not write to Apple Health. You can change permission in Apple Health or iOS Settings. Missing visible data is not treated as a definite denial or as zero.

Health reports and export previews are calculated locally. Daily JSON export can include the date, time zone, selected-source nutrition, medications, saved notes and workout/activity details: timestamps, duration, heart-rate minimum/average/maximum, HealthKit-estimated energy, distance, recognised PacePrompt planned and executed intervals, speed/inclination and their provenance, observed distance windows and optional native heart-rate zones, boundaries and source. It does not include raw heart-rate sample series or arbitrary HealthKit metadata. Availability varies by device, source and permission.

Sharing and Google Drive

Copying or sharing a report puts that content in the destination you select, such as the clipboard or a share-sheet recipient. Daily JSON Export lets you review a prepared snapshot and explicitly send those exact bytes to your chosen Google Drive destination. No automatic export, background sync or offline upload queue runs. The app talks directly to Google, using the limited drive.file scope, and reads back the exported bytes to check delivery. Google account/destination details, OAuth credentials and export identity are stored in this-device-only Keychain items. Signing out, disconnecting or revoking access stops future authorised use but does not delete files already in Drive. Delete those files separately in Drive, including its bin if appropriate. Google's storage, access and retention terms apply to your account and any people you share files with: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Local food records and notes

Food confirmations, intake history, personal-library items, favourites, aliases, quantities, draft food lists, inventory and imported evidence are stored locally. Receipt text you paste and text extracted from a PDF you select can be retained as evidence. Camera access is optional and used to scan food barcodes; the current app does not offer label-photo OCR upload. Offline food catalogues work without an online provider. If you explicitly confirm a web nutrition proposal, the app retains its displayed query, captured source text and quotations, source URL/hash, proposed values and your review decisions locally with the food record so its provenance can be inspected later. Inventory backup files are created only through an explicit export to a destination you choose; they are separate from the HealthKit JSON export.

The app-owned health-data directory, including notes, food databases, attachments and drafts, uses iOS file protection and is marked excluded from system backups before the app opens it. The app sets and verifies this exclusion request; the operating system controls backup and restore, so it is not an absolute guarantee that no copy can exist. It does not remove copies already made by older builds or files you exported elsewhere. If the directory cannot be protected, the affected local feature is unavailable rather than continuing without the protection. Review older backups and exported copies in the relevant Apple or storage-provider settings. Excluded local records may be lost if the device or app data is lost; there is no automatic cloud recovery.

Food-log removal is reversible: the app retains versioned history so an entry can be restored. It is not permanent erasure. There is currently no app-wide erase command. You can delete notes or discard drafts in the app. Unchanged saved notes from earlier days can be removed locally after the app verifies the same revision in Drive; drafts are retained. Deleting the app through iOS removes its app-container data, but exported files, earlier backups and Keychain items require separate attention. Remove your OpenRouter key and disconnect Drive before deleting the app if you want to remove those credentials; you can also revoke them at their providers. Older Gemini credentials are not migrated or used by this version; revoke any earlier Gemini key in Google AI Studio if you no longer need it. We cannot remotely read or erase your local records.

Optional online food search

Online food features are optional. Open Food Facts receives a barcode for an explicitly authorised lookup or the displayed terms of a submitted search when its search service is enabled. Web nutrition review requires your own OpenRouter API key, validation in the current session and an explicit Find and review nutrition or source-review action. Opening the review screen, typing food terms or storing a key does not submit a food query. Key validation contacts OpenRouter without food terms. A web search sends the displayed food terms through OpenRouter to its configured model and Exa search service. Source selection also uses returned page titles, URLs and excerpts. Supplying a public HTTPS source URL skips search and source selection.

The app fetches the chosen public page directly without sending the OpenRouter key to that publisher. Captured source text and displayed food terms go to OpenRouter and the configured extraction and applicability providers for structured proposals and a separate check against the requested food. The app does not attach HealthKit records, saved food or diary history, notes, receipts, photos or audio to these requests. Providers and publishers receive ordinary network information such as IP addresses and request details. Avoid entering private or sensitive information in food terms. To stay offline, keep optional database search disabled and avoid manual web review and barcode-lookup actions. Removing a credential cannot recall requests already sent or erase provider records.

Your OpenRouter key uses this-device-only Keychain storage. Search and model requests may incur charges on your account; set account/key limits at OpenRouter. Model requests require endpoints classified by OpenRouter for zero data retention and no data collection, with no provider fallback. These model-routing controls do not establish the retention policy of search services, publisher websites or OpenRouter's own account/usage records. Search-service terms may permit query data to be used for service improvement or model training; model-retention settings do not prevent that. Those services apply their own terms; the app cannot independently verify or erase their logs. The current app uses Azure-hosted Luna for discovery, source selection and applicability checking and xAI-hosted Grok for extraction. Jev ranking is not enabled in the default app flow. Read the applicable terms before using online review:

OpenRouter terms: https://openrouter.ai/terms
OpenRouter model retention controls and their scope: https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr

Voice

The app's optional note and food microphone paths request microphone/speech permission when invoked and require on-device speech recognition, with no server-recognition fallback. Audio is not saved, logged or uploaded by the app. Recognised text remains editable and is stored only through the relevant draft or confirmation flow. Ordinary iOS keyboard Dictation is a separate system feature governed by your Apple settings.

Permissions, diagnostics and policy changes

You can revoke Health, microphone, speech and camera permissions in Apple's settings, disable food-search services in the app, and revoke Google access in your Google account. No app analytics or tracking SDK is included. Apple may process TestFlight feedback, crash or diagnostic information under its own settings and terms; information you voluntarily send in feedback is separate from app data collection. Please avoid including health records or credentials in public bug reports. This policy is available offline in the app and at https://github.com/syamaner/WeeklyHealthReport/blob/main/docs/privacy-policy.txt. A later release may update it when its data flows change.
