WeeklyHealthReport privacy policy
Effective 22 November 2026

WeeklyHealthReport is a local health-reporting and food-recording app developed by Sertan Yamaner / qbq Solutions Ltd. Contact: proxy@sertan.com. We do not operate an app account service, analytics service, advertising system or backend that receives your health records. We do not sell your data, track you across apps, or use your health data for advertising or model training.

Apple Health

With your permission, the app reads selected HealthKit information to calculate reports on your iPhone. This can include body measurements, glucose, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, heart rate and variability, cardiorespiratory fitness, steps, walking/running distance, sleep, activity, workouts, selected dietary data and, on supported systems, individually selected medications. Access is read-only: the app does not write to Apple Health. You can change permission in Apple Health or iOS Settings. Missing visible data is not treated as a definite denial or as zero.

Health reports and export previews are calculated locally. Daily JSON export can include the date, time zone, selected-source nutrition, medications, saved notes and workout/activity details: timestamps, duration, heart-rate minimum/average/maximum, HealthKit-estimated energy, native distance and separately evidenced accepted treadmill distance, recognised PacePrompt planned and executed intervals, speed/inclination and their provenance, observed distance windows and optional native heart-rate zones, boundaries and source. For older PacePrompt workouts, the app can read the single matching associated distance record to recover its saved treadmill total without changing native statistics. The workout-detail option also includes available individual workout-associated heart-rate readings, their timestamps, BPM, sample and source identifiers, and executed interval references. It does not include arbitrary HealthKit metadata. Availability varies by device, source and permission. Glucose readings is a separate option that is off unless you switch it on. While it is on, the Daily JSON also includes each glucose reading Apple Health shows for the selected day, with its time, its value and the name and identifier of the app that recorded it. Readings that reach Apple Health later are included only if you export that day again. If you view the exact file and choose Copy JSON, the copied text is the whole file, including those readings; it is placed on this iPhone's clipboard only and expires after ten minutes.

Sharing and Google Drive

Copying or sharing a report puts that content in the destination you select, such as the clipboard or a share-sheet recipient. When you take a screenshot and choose Full Page, the app creates a PDF in memory for the iOS screenshot editor: on Health it contains the report; on Today it contains today's food totals, quick-add favourite names and amounts, today's note count and today-so-far Health values. If you choose Report a misread for a pasted nutrition label, the app shows a report containing the pasted label text, the values it read and the reader versions; you can edit it, and it goes only to the destination you pick in the share sheet, or nowhere if you cancel. Nothing is sent automatically; it leaves the device only if you save or share it. Daily JSON Export lets you review a prepared snapshot and explicitly send those exact bytes to your chosen Google Drive destination. With Food log on, the same Export also sends a separate food file for the selected day to that destination: the foods you logged, their amounts and times, nutrient values with their sources (for example a printed label, a food database or a web source page address) and the day's totals. For a value that is an estimate you approved in your own food database, the food file also says what kind of source the estimate came from, how it was carried over to your food, how its food state was established and how strong the match is, each as a fixed code word; for an estimate read from a web page it includes that public page address without any query string or fragment and, if a copy of the page was kept when your database was built, a checksum of that copy. It does not include pasted label text, receipts, photos, captured web page text or search queries. No automatic export, background sync or offline upload queue runs. The app talks directly to Google, using the limited drive.file scope, and reads back the exported bytes to check delivery. Google account/destination details, OAuth credentials and export identity are stored in this-device-only Keychain items. Signing out, disconnecting or revoking access stops future authorised use but does not delete files already in Drive. Delete those files separately in Drive, including its bin if appropriate. Google's storage, access and retention terms apply to your account and any people you share files with: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Local food records and notes

Food confirmations, intake history, personal-library items, favourites, aliases, quantities, draft food lists, inventory and imported evidence are stored locally. Receipt text you paste and text extracted from a PDF you select can be retained as evidence. Nutrition-label text you paste and then log is kept locally, unchanged, as the evidence for that food; it is read on your device and is not sent to any service. Camera access is optional and used to scan food barcodes and, if you choose, to photograph a nutrition label. If you log a food from a label photo, the cropped photo that was sent is kept locally with that entry as its evidence; it is not included in the Drive food file or in backups. Photos you do not log, and your original photos, are not stored by the app. Offline food catalogues work without an online provider. If you explicitly confirm a web nutrition proposal, the app retains its displayed query, captured source text and quotations, source URL/hash, proposed values and your review decisions locally with the food record so its provenance can be inspected later. If you log a food that has estimates you approved in your own food database, the app keeps with that entry a record of those estimates as they were offered: their values, the source, the food state the source describes, the state assumed for your food, the written assumption and the match strength, and which of them you left out. This record stays on your device, and its written descriptions are not included in the Drive food file. Inventory backup files are created only through an explicit export to a destination you choose; they are separate from the HealthKit JSON export and from the daily food file. Today's food totals and quick-add favourite names appear outside the app only in Today's Full Page, and only if you choose and then save or share it (see Sharing and Google Drive).

The app-owned health-data directory, including notes, food databases, attachments and drafts, uses iOS file protection and is marked excluded from system backups before the app opens it. The app sets and verifies this exclusion request; the operating system controls backup and restore, so it is not an absolute guarantee that no copy can exist. It does not remove copies already made by older builds or files you exported elsewhere. If the directory cannot be protected, the affected local feature is unavailable rather than continuing without the protection. Review older backups and exported copies in the relevant Apple or storage-provider settings. Excluded local records may be lost if the device or app data is lost; there is no automatic cloud recovery.

Food-log removal is reversible: the app retains versioned history so an entry can be restored. It is not permanent erasure. There is currently no app-wide erase command. You can delete notes or discard drafts in the app. Unchanged saved notes from earlier days can be removed locally after the app verifies the same revision in Drive; drafts are retained. Deleting the app through iOS removes its app-container data, but exported files, earlier backups and Keychain items require separate attention. Remove your OpenRouter key and disconnect Drive before deleting the app if you want to remove those credentials; you can also revoke them at their providers. Older Gemini credentials are not migrated or used by this version; revoke any earlier Gemini key in Google AI Studio if you no longer need it. We cannot remotely read or erase your local records.

Optional online food search

Online food features are optional. Open Food Facts receives a barcode for an explicitly authorised lookup or the displayed terms of a submitted search when its search service is enabled. To stay offline, keep optional database search disabled, keep OpenRouter turned off and avoid barcode-lookup actions.
Open Food Facts: https://world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy

OpenRouter

The app uses OpenRouter only if you turn on Use OpenRouter for food lookups, label reading and Confirm suggestions in Settings › OpenRouter. It is off by default. While it is off, the app sends nothing to OpenRouter: no lookup, no label photo, no key validation and no model check. Turning it off stops every further request; requests already sent cannot be recalled. It does not delete your key, which you remove separately with Remove key. Lookups, label reads and Confirm suggestions need your own OpenRouter API key, kept in this-device-only Keychain storage, without sync or backup migration, and validated in the current session. Requests may incur charges on your account; set account and key limits at OpenRouter.

While it is on, the app uses OpenRouter only in these ways, each only when you start it or turn it on:

Key validation. When you save or revalidate a key. Sends only the key, to check it; never food terms or any other data.

Web lookup. When you tap Look up or start a source review. Opening the screen, typing food terms or storing a key does not submit a food query. Each review uses at most one search request; for each source tried, one source-selection request, three website connections including redirects, one extraction request and one applicability check; and if the first source gives no usable values, one more suggested source may be tried in the same way, without a new search. The app has no monetary spending cap for web lookup; set credit limits at OpenRouter. A search sends the displayed food terms, tagged with the food market set in Settings when one is set, through OpenRouter to its configured model and the Exa search service; source selection also uses the returned page titles, addresses and excerpts. Supplying a public HTTPS source address skips search and source selection. The app fetches the chosen public page directly, without sending your key to that publisher. The captured page text and the displayed food terms then go to OpenRouter's configured extraction and applicability providers for a structured proposal and a separate check against the requested food. A page is sent whole up to a fixed size; for a larger page only its nutrition tables and lists, the lines and headings around them, its main heading and its structured data are sent, and the review says that only part of the page was read. When a page gives more than one set of values for the same food, those sets are also sent for the check. Never sent: Apple Health records, saved food or diary history, notes, receipts, photos or audio. If a model request fails for a temporary reason, such as a lost connection, a timeout or a busy service, the app may send the same request again, unchanged, at most twice; it never retries fetches of publisher pages. The current app uses Azure-hosted Luna for discovery, source selection and applicability checking and xAI-hosted Grok for extraction; Jev ranking is not enabled in the default flow. Avoid entering private or sensitive information in food terms.

Label photo reading. Available only in internal test builds. When you tap Read label with AI. The photo may be one you took, chose from your library or pasted on the Nutrition label screen; the app reads the clipboard only when you tap Paste on that screen. Sends the part of the photo you cropped, made smaller and re-encoded with location, camera and other metadata removed, with a fixed instruction; OpenRouter passes it to one named model endpoint (currently Gemini 4 Argon on Google Vertex) with no fallback to other providers. Before the first read of a session and of each day, and again after a check that could not connect, two catalogue requests with no key and no data confirm that endpoint. Never sent: your food name, brand, food log, notes or Apple Health data. The model's answer pre-fills the label values, which you check and correct before anything is saved. A failed request is retried at most twice within the same tap, and reading is limited to 30 photos and $0.50 of reported cost a day. Avoid photographing anything other than the nutrition table.

Confirm suggestions. Only if you also turn on Suggest missing food details and values on Confirm in Settings › OpenRouter. When the food you are confirming has unknown food details or unknown nutrient values, the app asks automatically, a moment after the food is chosen. It never asks in the background, from search results, on the quick add sheet, when you log again from an earlier entry or a favourite, when you reopen a log or update its nutrition from your database, or for web lookups, label readings or foods from Your foods. Sends a description of that food as its source gives it (never words you typed in a correction): the name and brand, a composition table's variant text, and for your own food database its category, product type and state where the app has them (a pack size written in a name is sent with the name), the food details already known, which source the food comes from (for example a food composition table), the questions asked or the nutrients wanted and, for nutrient estimates, the nutrition basis and the known values. A name from your own food database can come from a receipt product title, so check names you would not want sent. Food details go to the Jev model through OpenRouter's System One service; nutrient estimates go to one named model endpoint (currently GPT-6.1 Sol on Azure) with no fallback to other providers. You can also tap Ask again on Confirm to send the same request once more, within the daily limit. Never sent: the search text you typed, words you typed in a correction, the amount you ate, the time or date, notes, other entries, Apple Health data, barcodes, identifiers, photos or prices. Suggested food details are filled in for you to check and change; estimated values are marked est. and you can leave out each one before you tap Log. Nothing is added to your log until you tap Log, and an estimate never replaces a value from a label or a database. A failed request is retried at most twice; suggestions are limited to 100 requests and $0.50 of reported cost a day, and answers are kept on your device, so the same food is usually not sent again (a tap of Ask again, a change of details before you log or an answer older than 30 days sends again). OpenRouter keeps its own account and usage records for these requests, including the model, token counts, cost and time, under its terms. If you log a food with an estimated value, the Drive food file names the model that estimated it.

Other uses are added to this list only in the release that starts them, after their own approval.

For every use: model requests require endpoints classified by OpenRouter for zero data retention and no data collection, with no provider fallback. These controls do not establish the retention policy of search services, publisher websites or OpenRouter's own account and usage records, which include the model, token counts, cost and time of each request, kept under OpenRouter's terms. Search-service terms may permit query data to be used for service improvement or model training; model-retention settings do not prevent that. Providers and publishers receive ordinary network information such as IP addresses and request details. Those services apply their own terms; the app cannot independently verify or erase their logs. Requests already sent cannot be recalled, and removing your key or turning OpenRouter off cannot erase provider records. Read the applicable terms before turning it on:

Voice

The app's optional note and food microphone paths request microphone/speech permission when invoked and require on-device speech recognition, with no server-recognition fallback. Audio is not saved, logged or uploaded by the app. Recognised text remains editable and is stored only through the relevant draft or confirmation flow. Ordinary iOS keyboard Dictation is a separate system feature governed by your Apple settings.

Permissions, diagnostics and policy changes

You can revoke Health, microphone, speech and camera permissions in Apple's settings, disable food-search services in the app, and revoke Google access in your Google account. No app analytics or tracking SDK is included. Apple may process TestFlight feedback, crash or diagnostic information under its own settings and terms; information you voluntarily send in feedback is separate from app data collection. Please avoid including health records or credentials in public bug reports. This policy is available offline in the app and at https://github.com/syamaner/WeeklyHealthReport/blob/main/docs/privacy-policy.txt. A later release may update it when its data flows change.

Private evaluation tooling outside the app

For the owner's explicitly requested personal food database evaluation review, selected product titles, matched record names and preparation states may be supplied to the model provider of the coding agent doing the review (Codex or Claude Code) to propose identity and food-state judgements. Row keys align those proposals with the private review sheet. This permission is limited to that review: it excludes query sets and expected answers, nutrient values, pack verdicts, private configuration, unrelated notes and held-out contents. Proposals remain private and provisional, and the owner validates them; the amendment below adds nutrient evidence and removes pack comparisons from this review. The app does not perform this transfer.


Evaluation best-estimate amendment (8 November 2026)

For the explicitly requested estimate review, food descriptions may be sent to web search and
source sites, with national composition databases and manufacturer pages preferred. Receipt text
and personal data are excluded. Source nutrient values, food state and provenance may enter the
model context for this work. This narrowly supersedes the earlier evaluation nutrient-context and
external-query exclusions. Private outputs retain source URL, date read and page_unconfirmed status;
no catalogue or evaluation file is published, and no app transfer or automatic food save is added.
