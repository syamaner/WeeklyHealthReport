# Gemini grounded discovery development pilot, 28 September 2026

This is a partial development-case diagnostic, not a score of the 40-case v2 set, an app acceptance result or a nutrition source-admission decision. All calls used the locally authorised Mac Keychain credential without recording it. The private collector retained only projected response text, citation annotations and attempt status in owner-only `/private/tmp` directories. No v2 holdout was sent to Gemini. The app prompt and request configuration have not been changed.

The bullets above are selective checks of visible answers and selected pages, not independent judgements for every annotation. Several citations repeat the same destination. The v1 scorer counted citation annotations as "leads", so its top-three metric could count one page three times. A v2 judgement now records each reviewed final destination URL, groups duplicate citations for page-level top-three results and retains citation-level support counts. It still requires all cases and independent page review before issuing a quality score.

## Later prompt gate

V5 constrained the response to food-source pages and unresolved distinctions; V6 further reduced it to a source list. Both completed all five requests at low thinking, but neither cleared the development pilot. V5 again attached unrequested nutrition and did not consistently preserve the specific food's identity. In V6, the printed USDA egg and milk links were cited to unrelated domains such as `rawpawiq.com` and `sniglobal.org`; the printed Kolios and Kraft Heinz manufacturer paths did not match the checkable manufacturer paths linked above. The fish-and-chips answer included a portal instead of a direct whole-dish record, and the Heinz answer omitted label/formulation-date uncertainty. These are visible source-attribution and identity failures, not a claim that the third-party pages themselves are false.

The offline [`diagnose_citations.py`](diagnose_citations.py) check flagged 8 of V6's 20 citation annotations as apparent written-link/citation-host mismatches (two in milk and six in eggs). It flagged two of V5's 20 as apparent mismatches, plus six model-written opaque Google redirects whose destinations cannot be checked from their host. These are syntactic diagnostics, not supported-citation or relevance scores. Synthetic tests cover matching subdomains, unrelated hosts, broad spans and opaque redirects.

Google's [Interactions grounding documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search) describes URL citations as annotations to text spans, not verification that a model-written Markdown URL in that span is the cited destination or that the page proves the entire claim. The [Generate Content grounding format](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/google-search) likewise maps text spans to web chunks; changing endpoint alone would not make page claims trustworthy. This is an inference from the documented response contracts and the pilot, not a measured comparison of endpoints.

## Decision

Do not adopt v2 through v6 or the low-thinking configuration in the app on this evidence. A full 28-case run of a candidate that already fails this five-case gate would add cost without making it eligible for selection. The next engineering step is a deterministic citation-to-displayed-link consistency check and a presentation that clearly separates provider citation URLs from model-written links and claims. Test that boundary with synthetic fixtures before another live pilot. Once a candidate clears the pilot, run the complete, page-reviewed development comparison using distinct-page metrics and case-specific identity/preparation checks, then inspect the untouched holdout once. No web finding in this pilot authorises adding nutrition data to the app.
